How to Use MonkeyType

MonkeyType is an exam for AI auditors. Each fictional company has books with planted accounting errors, or none at all. Here you take the same exam yourself, or inspect how the test policies did.

All companies and numbers are made up. You don’t need an account, and nothing you do is saved after you leave the page.

Start Here

  1. Open the site. If nobody has used it for a while, the first load can take about a minute while the free server wakes up.
  2. Use a laptop or desktop if you can. It works on a phone, but the tables are easier to read on a big screen.
  3. Pick what you want to do: play the auditor (about 5–10 minutes) or look at the reviewer dashboard (about 2 minutes).
Short on time? Choose Talaris Apparel LLC and press Submit Findings straight away without adding anything. That company is clean, so an empty answer is correct, and you’ll see how results work in under a minute.

Play the Auditor

Your goal: find the planted errors in one company’s books, prove each one with records, and submit. Planted errors are counted as correct; anything else counts as a false alarm.

  1. On the first page, choose a set with the bar above the list: Dev is a good default. Then press Audit → next to a company, or Random Company →.
  2. Open the records you need and look for things that don’t add up (see the four errors).
  3. For each error, build a finding in the Draft pane and press Add Finding →.
  4. Press Submit Findings → at the top. You get your score, a verdict on every finding, the answer key, and how the five test policies did on the same company.

Rules is worth reading first. It explains this company’s reporting period, which exchange rate to use, how rounding works, and which account number means what (receivables, bank, unapplied and so on).

Amounts are shown with decimals in each currency’s own format: KZT and EUR have 2 decimals, JPY none, KWD 3.

Steps: What Costs What

Like an AI agent, you have 100 steps. Each look at the books uses one, and each step also takes 0.002 off your final score, so be efficient but don’t be stingy.

When 10 steps are left the budget bar turns orange. If you reach the limit without submitting, the game ends with an empty answer, so submit before you run out.

The Four Errors

Every planted error is one of these four. A company can have several, or none.

ErrorWhat it looks likeAmount to enter
Duplicate invoiceThe same invoice is booked twice: two ledger rows debit receivables for the same invoice number.The extra booking, usually the invoice total.
Payment not appliedA payment lists the invoices it pays, but the ledger puts some or all of it in the unapplied account instead.The amount that went to unapplied, in the base currency.
Wrong currency conversionA foreign-currency invoice or payment was converted at the wrong rate or date. Check it against FX Rates using the rule on the Rules tab.Correct amount minus booked amount, in the base currency.
Wrong reporting periodAn invoice dated inside the period is booked outside it, or the other way round. Watch time zones: the company’s local date counts.Usually the amount that was booked.

Not everything unusual is an error. Split payments, refunds and repeated round amounts can be perfectly legitimate, and they’re there to tempt you.

Record a Finding

  1. Click the invoice or payment that is wrong, then press Mark as Affected in the Inspect pane. That also cites it as evidence and sets the currency to the base currency.
  2. Click each record that proves the error, such as the ledger rows or the exchange rate, and press Cite as Evidence. Cited rows get a small dot in the table. You can cite up to 10 records.
  3. Open the Draft pane. Choose the Error type, then type the Amount as a normal number such as 225.54. The hint underneath shows exactly what will be sent.
  4. Optionally write an Explanation. It helps you remember what you found, but it isn’t scored.
  5. Press Add Finding →. It moves to the Findings pane, where you can Edit or Delete it until you submit. The maximum is 30 findings.

You can also type an ID into the affected or evidence box and press Enter. These are the IDs the grader expects:

RecordUse this IDLooks like
Invoice or paymentIts Document ID, not the invoice or payment numberFILE-TTTDVMC
Ledger rowIts Row IDTX-HT49KFD5
Exchange rateFX: date, from-currency, to-currencyFX:2026-11-14:EUR:KZT

A finding counts as correct only if the type, the affected document, the currency and the amount all match a planted error, and your evidence includes every record needed to prove it.

Submit and Read Results

Press Submit Findings → and confirm. If you think the company is clean, submit with no findings: that is the right answer for a clean company.

The results page starts with one sentence, for example “You found 1 of 2 planted errors with 1 false alarm, for a reward of −0.006.” Below it:

  • Reward runs from −1 to 1. It rewards correct findings, penalises false alarms and missed errors, and takes 0.002 off for every step you used. A structurally broken answer scores −1.
  • You against the baseline policies: your reward next to five test policies on the same company. You’re highlighted in orange. oracle (dashed) reads the answer key, so it always scores 1. reconcile is the reference checker that knows every accounting rule. simple only looks for obvious patterns, so it misses currency and period errors. random guesses, and mark_all accuses everything.
  • Your Findings: each one is marked Correct, False alarm or Duplicate, with the reason when it’s wrong, e.g. “Right error and document, but the amount is wrong.”
  • Answer Key: every planted error, its amount and the records that prove it, marked Found or Missed.

Press Play Another Company → to try again.

Worked Example

Show a correct finding (spoiler)

Company Telum Industrial FZ-LLC has a currency-conversion error in one invoice:

  1. Open Invoices → Open First 50 Rows. Type FILE-TTTDVMC in the filter, click the row, then press Mark as Affected.
  2. In Draft, choose Wrong currency conversion and enter 225.54 KZT.
  3. Type TX-HT49KFD5 in the evidence box and press Enter, then FX:2026-11-14:EUR:KZT and Enter.
  4. Press Add Finding →, then Submit Findings →. This finding is marked Correct. The company has a second error; finding it is up to you.

Reviewer Dashboard

The dashboard shows how the five test policies performed and lets you check every claim against the records. It’s read-only: nothing you click changes anything. Open it with Reviewer Dashboard at the top of the site.

The Bar at the Top

Source picks the data: the short Demo (a control company with two errors plus a clean one) or Train and dev (all five policies on 20 companies). With the second one, Split switches between the 10 train companies and the 10 dev companies, and Mode switches between closed and train. Scores are the same in both modes; in train mode the Activity log also shows the reward at the last step. Where there is only one option, it is shown as plain text.

Benchmark

  • The sentence at the top is the headline result. Trace Its Findings → takes you straight to the evidence behind it.
  • The bar chart shows each policy’s average reward on an axis from −1 to 1. The orange bar is the reference policy; the dashed one is the oracle.
  • The table adds F1, precision, recall, steps and time. Click a policy name to see its runs. Metric Definitions explains each number, and Full Precision shows the unrounded values.

Runs

Every run is one policy on one company. Filter by episode ID, policy or status, and click a column header to sort. Click an episode ID to open that run.

A Single Run

  • Top: the outcome, F1, reward and steps, then TP (correct), FP (false alarms) and FN (missed). Other Policies on … compares every policy on the same company.
  • Findings: click a finding to see its evidence trail, a box for the finding with a line to each record it cites. Click a record to see its fields and linked records, then use ← Back to Finding. Copy ID copies the ID.
  • Activity: every step the policy took, in order, and what each read contained (for example which cited ledger rows and entries were on that page). The Agent’s View box at the top says how the policy was isolated and shows the result of an automatic scan proving no answer-key data reached it. Rewards show as 0 during a closed run on purpose; the final score appears only at the end.
  • Duplicate invoices are shown as postings: each posting is one ledger entry with its debit and credit line, and the evidence trail labels them “Posting 1 of 2”, “Posting 2 of 2”.
  • Episode Records: browse the company’s full ledger, invoices, payments, FX rates and accounting rules.

Robustness and Verification

  • Robustness: every company also exists as a twin with its rows shuffled and every ID renamed. This page puts each original next to its twin and confirms every policy scored them identically.
  • Verification: which stages of the official check ran, what each covers and how many tests ran. In the official run a skipped stage counts as a failure.

Your browser’s Back and Forward buttons work everywhere, and you can copy the address to share the exact view you’re looking at.

If Something Goes Wrong

You seeWhat to do
The page takes a minute to loadThe free server was asleep. Wait; it’s fast once awake.
“Unknown or expired session”The server restarted (for example after sleeping), so the game in progress is gone. Go back to the start page and pick the company again.
Your progress disappeared after reloadingGames live only in the open page. Reloading or leaving starts over, and the browser warns you first.
“Baseline scores unavailable on this host”The comparison couldn’t be computed on this server. Your own score is still correct.
A finding you’re sure about is marked “False alarm”Read the reason under it. The most common causes are a wrong amount, a missing evidence record, or a number used instead of a Document ID.
The dashboard says “No Evaluation Loaded”The server is still preparing its data after waking. Reload in a minute.

Glossary

LedgerThe company’s book of accounts: every money movement as debit and credit rows.
InvoiceA bill the company sent a customer.
PaymentMoney received, with a list of the invoices it pays.
FX rateThe exchange rate for one currency into the base currency on one date.
Base currencyThe currency the company reports in, shown at the top of the screen.
ReceivablesMoney customers owe the company. Each invoice should add to it exactly once.
UnappliedA holding account for payment money not matched to any invoice.
EvidenceThe records that prove a finding. The grader needs the complete set.
F1One number from 0 to 1 that balances errors found against false alarms. 1 is perfect.
RewardThe final score from −1 to 1: based on F1, minus a small cost per step.
PolicyA strategy an automated agent follows. The site compares five test policies.
